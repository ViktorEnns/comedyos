Stand up is the journey to finding out who you are not. 

when a sculptor "removes the stone... the sculpture is what’s left—that’s what a stand up is" 

Seinfeld views the work of stand-up as a path to defining oneself by exclusion:
• He characterized standup as a "journey of finding out who you are who you're not".
The Sculpting Metaphor
To explain this concept, Seinfeld compares the stand-up process not to construction, but to subtraction:
• He explains that stand-up is "not building something you’re taking things away".
• He directly compares the comedian to a sculptor: when a sculptor "removes the stone... the sculpture is what’s left—that’s what a stand up is".
Rejecting False Identity
Because stand-up is this lifelong process of defining the self, Seinfeld notes that comedians inherently reject the idea of adopting other personas:
• He stated that comedians "hate" Halloween.
• The reason for this aversion is that the "last thing that’s fun to us is to be somebody else," despite how hard they have worked on their identity.
• He remarked on the irony of avoiding costumes, saying he does not want to pretend to be characters like Dracula, yet jokingly conceded that being himself is "way worse... than being Jerry Seinfeld or whomever".


Comedy as True Consciousness

For Seinfeld, the stage is the place of true existence and connection, contrasting sharply with conventional life:
• He described his engagement in stand-up as being "locked in" and "connected to something".
• In contrast, he suggested that the "real life that people live when we’re not doing comedy" is like a "diorama" (a scene in a box).
• He noted that comedians have a "split Consciousness" where roughly 70% is comedy and only 30% relates to "real life," reinforcing that everything outside the craft is a "form of Disconnect"


100m dash and comedy are similar: 
can't fake it

it's scary. you're breaking a human being down to nothing. there are no tricks in your pocket, nothing to help you. 

Direct Connection: The Purity of the Effort

Seinfeld relates the 100-meter dash to stand-up comedy in three words: "Can't fake it".
He explains that both require the performer to be utterly exposed and focused on excellence:
• Breaking Down the Human: In both the 100-meter dash and stand-up, you are "breaking a human being down to nothing".
• The Lack of Help: For a sprinter, there is "nothing to help you" when the gun is shot. Similarly, in stand-up, you have to "destroy them for an hour and... got nothing in your pocket your pockets are empty".
• Excellence and Purity: Seinfeld is "obsessed with" and "animated by" the pursuit of excellence. He states that he is drawn to "Purity and quality which are the same thing to me". The 100-meter dash, being the ultimate test of speed where "we're gonna find out" who is the fastest, embodies this purity. He identifies sprinters as the analogy for the most exciting stand-up comedians: "Rickles and Rodney... were 100 meter sprinters".
The Immediate, High-Stakes Nature
Both activities are immediate, high-stakes contests that require full engagement:
• The Moment is Now: In sprinting, the challenge is "now, right go yeah go".
• It's Scary: Lining up for the dash is described as "scary". Similarly, stand-up begins at 8:00 PM, where you have "5,000 people and... you got to destroy them for an hour".
The discipline required for comedy is comparable to athletic training. He mentioned that in the past, he and a friend "used to run used to like run like you’re training for a fight"

Analogy: Seinfeld's comparison suggests that stand-up, like the 100-meter dash, is the purest form of its discipline—a stripped-down, all-out effort where the slightest imperfection or lack of commitment is immediately visible, much like trying to find "who's the fastest guy really"
