# Lululemon’s stock is down 62%. Nike’s struggling.

Lululemon’s stock is down 62%. Nike’s struggling. The brands that owned a generation are playing to empty stadiums.

Here’s what happened: the crowd grew up and stopped believing the show was real.

Gen Z cut spending by 13% this year. Four out of five wait for sales. Brand loyalty? Dead. 81% switched brands last year, not for something better, but because loyalty itself feels like getting played.

That TikTok “de-influencing” trend? That was a generation saying: Stop buying sh*t you don’t need.

This isn’t a recession thing. The kids aren’t broke. They’re awake.

They watched 2008. They lived through COVID. They’ve seen their parents buried in stuff that didn’t make them happy. They’ve watched influencers lie and brands pretend to care until the tweet cycle ended.

They learned the lesson we forgot: buying things doesn’t fix anything.

For us, consumption was religion. You were someone who wore Nikes. My kids don’t pray there anymore. And most brands are still preaching to empty pews.

Gen Z isn’t anti-brand. They’re anti-bullsh*t. They grew up inside the marketing machine. They’ve seen every trick. When everything is marketing, nothing gets trusted.

Here’s the part that should terrify every CEO: they’re not replacing consumption with apathy. They’re replacing it with peace.

The generation raised on Instagram doesn’t want to impress you. They want to ignore you.

Think about that. The death of showing off is the death of half the consumer economy.

We built empires on three assumptions, all wrong. People want more stuff. Wrong, they want fewer things that matter. People care what others think. Wrong, they care what they think. People stay loyal. Wrong, they’re gone the second you slip.

I run a legacy business, but I’m changing how I lead. I’m asking: How do we mean more instead of sell more? How do we earn trust instead of buy attention?

I’m cutting initiatives to focus on fewer things done with real intention. I’m prioritizing proof over promises. And I’m listening to customers who left, younger employees who see what’s coming, and my own kids.

Most brands operate like it’s 1997. Consumers live like it’s 2035. The next generation figured out what we refused to admit: most of what we sold was bullsh*t.

This isn’t the death of consumerism. It’s the birth of something smarter. An economy where value beats vanity. Where meaning beats marketing. Where you either matter or you disappear.

The question isn’t whether this is happening. It’s whether you’ll adapt before you become irrelevant.
